Sala, kitchen and parking

A first layout read from the developer plan. One thing still needs checking on site before this becomes final: where the kitchen counter actually sits.

Sala

The sala sits in the front half of the house, facing the right party wall. That wall gets an oak panel with a 50 inch TV and a floating console. A 1.8 m sofa faces it about 1.1 to 1.5 m away, with its back to the walkway that runs from the front door to the kitchen.

Refrigerator

Put it at the end of the counter, with its back to the party wall and the door opening toward the dining table. It stays within a step of the sink, never blocks the service-area door or the toilet, and you can grab a drink without walking into the cooking zone.

The service area is the fallback, but heat, humidity and rain exposure shorten a fridge's life there, so use it only if it is fully roofed.

Dining

A 90 cm round table with four chairs sits right in front of the kitchen, so serving is a step away. It keeps about 1 m between the table edge and the sink counter. A round table has no corners for the kids to bump into, and the fridge door still clears the nearest chair.

Entry

A 30 cm deep shoe cabinet goes on the inside wall of the entry pocket. That leaves about 90 cm to walk in past the door.

Parking

The subdivision allows the car to extend about 1.2 m past the lot line instead of parking on the road, so the Vios parks nose-in in the 3.65 m bay beside the entry. That leaves about 90 cm for each door.

  • A rubber wheel stop about 1 m from the front wall keeps the bumper clear of the wall and window.
  • Park nose-in. The driver's door then opens on the entry side, toward the walkway, and the trunk faces the street for groceries.
  • Keep the 1.35 m strip in front of the entry clear as the walkway to the door.

Gate and side walls

Full-height walls now close both sides of the carport up to the canopy. On the lot line, from left to right:

  • A 90 cm pedestrian gate on the walkway. It swings inward, so you can come and go without opening the car gate.
  • A 20 cm pillar with stone cladding, the mailbox, the house number and a wall light.
  • A 3-leaf telescopic sliding gate for the car. The leaves stack behind a 90 cm fixed panel, so the 2.6 m opening needs no space to swing and no long track along the frontage. It is motor-ready.

All panels share one look: a black powder-coated steel frame with horizontal aluminium slats in the same oak tone as the carport ceiling, spaced for air and a little privacy. The Vios now parks centred on the opening, with about 60 cm on the passenger side.

Because the car extends past the lot line, the gate stays open while it is parked and closes when the car is out. Indicative cost: ₱90,000 – 180,000 for the gates, ₱40,000 – 65,000 for the side walls, ₱10,000 – 20,000 for pillar cladding, and ₱25,000 – 45,000 for an optional gate motor. These are planning numbers, not quotes.

Carport roof

The second floor already overhangs the front by about 1.9 m. A new flat canopy adds the last 1.6 m out to the lot line across the full 5 m frontage, so the entry, the walkway and most of the car stay dry. The car's front 1.2 m past the lot line stays uncovered.

  • Black steel frame, hung below the second-floor band so it stays under the bedroom window sills. Two slim posts at the lot-line corners.
  • Pre-painted long-span metal roofing on top, sloped about 3% toward the street to a gutter and a downspout on the side post.
  • Wood-look PVC or WPC soffit under both the overhang and the canopy, with 5 warm downlights, matching the bedrooms' oak tone.
  • Optional black slatted screens on the two side walls for privacy, as in the photo.

Indicative cost: ₱60,000 – 115,000 for the canopy frame and roofing, soffit panels under both areas, gutter, lights and permit fees. Side screens add ₱15,000 – 30,000. These are planning numbers, not quotes.

Check before building the roof

  • Get written approval from Pinnacle Homes or the homeowners' association. Canopies to the lot line are often allowed, but the rules vary by subdivision.
  • Apply for a building permit from the municipal engineering office. A roofed carport counts as an addition.
  • Ask for a fire-rated or non-combustible finish on the party-wall sides if the association requires it.
